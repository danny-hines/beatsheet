# Prompting each model

How each model wants its prompt, beyond what SKILL.md says for every clip. From each maker's guide where there is one, fal's model pages, and tested community guides; the evidence and sources are in `docs/MODELS.md`. Read the section for the model you're about to use.

## For every video model

- From a board, say what moves, not what the board shows: restating the picture gives a near-static clip or a face that drifts. (MiniMax H3 is the exception, below.)
- One camera move, with a pace word ("slow push in", "quick whip pan"). When it shouldn't move, say "locked-off camera"; with no direction, most models drift. Orbits of 90–180° in a short clip, never 360°; two stacked moves lose the second.
- About one beat per two seconds. Too much action for the length gets rushed, dropped or cut into shots of its own.
- Dialogue: quotation marks around the spoken words and nowhere else (a word quoted for emphasis gets said aloud), a speaker and a tone before each line, at most about 2.5 words a second. A line that's too long for the clip comes out fast or slips out of sync.
- Sound: the cut's music, voice and sfx are the real soundtrack, so ask for what's heard in the shot and no score: "Sound: rain on the awning, distant traffic; no music." Say what's there rather than only what isn't.
- Real lettering doesn't survive a video model. Put it in a coded overlay, or on the board and animate gently.
- What a model won't draw is part of choosing it, so pick one whose rules allow the shot before you send it. When one refuses anyway, rephrase what's in the shot or move that kind of shot to a model that takes it (see SKILL.md). Don't hide faces or rename things to get it past the same filter.

## In the catalog

**Kling 2.5 Turbo and Kling 3 Pro (fal).** Two to five short sentences: who moves, how, the camera, the atmosphere. From a board, motion and camera only, and always name a move, or Kling holds mostly static. Clips of 3–6 seconds hold together better than long ones. Neither makes sound here (Kling 3 is sent with its own sound off), so leave dialogue and sound out.

**Wan 2.2 (fal at 720p, or the 14B and 5B on the person's card).** 80–120 words in the order the camera sees things: shot size and angle, the subject, the action, the background. One shot size and one move, about 5 seconds' worth. Describing the foreground moving against the background gives parallax. No sound.

**MiniMax H3, H3 Max and H3 Max Turbo (fal).** Start from the board: a sentence on its style, who's in it and where, then what happens next, in order. Keep who they are, what they wear and where things stand as the board has them. Write camera moves as part of the action ("she turns as the camera eases in"), not tacked on at the end. It makes its own sound, so write the sound in: the place's sound in a sentence or two, and "no music" or a description of the music you do want. For dialogue, describe each voice the first time it speaks ("a young woman, quiet and breathy, says: …"); for voiceover, say it's off-screen and her lips stay closed. fal rewrites the prompt into H3's own structure by default, so plain sentences work. With references (`refs`, no board), beatsheet adds a line saying the pictures are references for who and what is in the shot; refer to them as "Image 1", "Image 2" in the order given, each with a job ("the woman in Image 1 walks her dog from Image 2"), and say which wins if two disagree. H3 isn't strong at big action or steady faces; for a fight or a close-up, see `docs/MODELS.md`. H3 Max Turbo makes 5 seconds at least, and a take is back in about 12 seconds.

**MiniMax H3 on the person's card (ComfyUI).** Nothing rewrites the prompt here, and MiniMax's [prompt guide](https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3/blob/main/docs/VIDEO_PROMPT_WRITING_GUIDE_base_en.md) wants its own structure: three labelled fields, `integrated_multimodal_description:` (`[Shot 1]` first, later shots with a cut time like `[Shot 2] At 00:03.500, …`), `overall_soundscape:` and `non_diegetic_music:` (`N/A` for none), with dialogue as `(S1) says: <d>[English] the words</d>`. From a board, the guide puts an instruction line first that names the board `<Picture 1>` as the frame at 0.00 seconds. Its license doesn't cover the US, EU, UK or South Korea (`docs/MODELS.md`).

**Wan 3.0 (fal).** Name a shot size with the move ("medium close-up, slight low angle, slow push in"), and say "one continuous shot": it cuts on its own (a four-word prompt came back as five cuts in five seconds). References by position ("the woman in Image 1"). Lines in quotes with the emotion, tone and pace, about two short sentences per 5 seconds. Say what you want to hear, or it picks its own soundtrack; its mix runs loud. beatsheet leaves fal's prompt expansion on (fal says turning it off lowers quality; whether a full prompt does better without it isn't tested). A rough is 480p: judge the motion and timing in it, not the detail.

**Seedance 2.5 (fal).** The first 20–30 words carry the most weight: open with the subject and the core action. Say "one continuous shot, no cuts": it cuts on its own. Quote every spoken line with a speaker label and a tone; dialogue written as plain prose comes back as burned-in subtitles. State the camera distance for small subjects, and the contact points and what follows (the leaves scatter, the mug tips). With references (`refs`), beatsheet adds a line naming them @Image1, @Image2…; give each a job in that order ("@Image1 is Mia's face and wardrobe; @Image2 is the red bicycle"). Its 480p rough and 1080p final are made separately (finishing the draft by its `draft_id` isn't wired yet), so the final can differ from the rough. On fal it won't draw real people; the fit check rules it out for them there. Through Tein it does, and the same prompting holds, with two differences: references are named in plain words rather than @Image1 (beatsheet adds a line saying the reference pictures show who and what is in it, so say which is which by what's in each), and a take that starts from a board goes without its references, since Tein doesn't take both together.

**Kling 4.0 (fal).** As Kling 3: two to five sentences, motion and camera from a board, always a named move. It's the one for an orbit or a crane move, and for a long take that has to hit its beats: say when each happens ("at about 10 seconds she turns"). Unlike Kling 3 it makes its own sound (fal has no switch for it). No seed, so budget an extra take. A take runs about five minutes and sometimes fifteen, at about 16¢ a second, so agree a long take's staging first (see SKILL.md).

**Gemini Omni Flash (fal).** The camera first, then the subject, action, setting and light. Say "one continuous shot". `[0-3s]` style timecodes place beats, on the music for instance. Leaving things out can be said plainly ("no dialogue"). Wide or tall only: a square or 4:5 shot comes back 16:9 or 9:16, to be reframed. It won't draw real people.

**SCAIL-2 and Wan 2.2 Animate.** See Copying movement in SKILL.md: long and specific, who each person is and wears, the place, the light, and "static camera" when the clip's camera holds.

**Kling Motion Control (fal).** The board is who and where; the clip is how they move, and the take is framed as the clip is. So make the board in the clip's shape, with the same number of people in the same places as its first frame. A short prompt for the scene is enough ("two knights duel in a castle courtyard at dusk"). The clip's own sound is left out.

**Lip sync models.** See Lip sync in SKILL.md. The words come from the sound, never the prompt: don't quote the line. sync-3, Lipsync 2 Pro, Kling Lip Sync, LatentSync, H3 Max Lip Sync and VEED Fabric take no prompt at all, so the board (or the clip) is everything: a face turned toward the camera, the mouth clear of hands and props, eyes open (H3 Max Lip Sync keeps a board's closed eyes closed for the whole take). LatentSync wants a face toward the camera most of all, and does worst on stylized or non-human characters. Kling Avatar 2 Pro, OmniHuman 2, LongCat Avatar and InfiniteTalk read the shot's prompt for how the person moves while talking ("leans in, speaking quietly, small hand gestures, static camera"); keep it to manner and camera. One person speaks per take; a board with two faces moves whichever the model picks.

**GPT Image (stills).** Two flavours: GPT Image (`gpt-image`, 2.6 Flare: quick, for boards) and GPT Image Sunburst (`gpt-image-sunburst`: for people and likeness, character sheets, products, lettering, and edits that must keep what's there). Both prompt the same way. Order the prompt: the background or place, the subject, the details, then the constraints. Say what it's for ("a storyboard frame for a 9:16 video"). Label the pictures it's given ("Image 1: Mia's face") and, when editing, repeat what must stay the same. Medium quality for boards; high for lettering, faces and real people. It refuses fights and blood with people from real photos, so keep those shots out of its passes (see SKILL.md). On Higgsfield both flavours are 2.5, the version before.

**Soul 2 (stills, on Higgsfield only).** From a prompt alone: it takes no reference pictures here, so it can't keep a cast member or a place from their sheets. Use it for stills with no one from the cast in them, and GPT Image there for the rest. Its prompt goes as written (Higgsfield's own rewriting is off). Nothing here has compared it with the others yet.

**FLUX 3 Image Klein (stills, on the person's card).** Nothing rewrites the prompt here (BFL's prompt upsampler isn't used), so write the whole caption of the picture you want rather than an instruction: the place, the subject and what they're doing, the light, the style, with any change to a reference folded in. Say what to take from each reference by its place ("the man from the first picture, in the coat from the second"); beatsheet adds which picture is whom.

**Nano Banana 2.1 and the first Nano Banana (stills).** Full sentences, as if briefing an artist. 2.1 takes up to 14 pictures, but only 4 of them people: a board with more people than that keeps the first four best. To keep someone, name the picture to keep them from. When a board is nearly right, edit it rather than make it again, but go back to the original sheet each time rather than chaining edits of edits: every still model drifts as it's edited again and again.

**Character sheets, for every still model** (two tips from creators, not yet tested here): a mid-gray background, because white blows out when the sheet is used as a reference; and the face once, with the full-body view kept clear, so the video model isn't handed several faces to choose from.

**LTX-2.5.** One paragraph in the present tense, starting with the action, 4–8 sentences, under 200 words. Light as a source with a direction. From a board, only the change, ending on how things finish. No shot lists, no "Camera:" labels, no negatives.
